# Trading Simulator: Product Spec v1

Nov 27, 2026 · Prafull Kotecha

## Summary

An app for iPhone, Android and the web (an installable PWA) that teaches anyone 13 or older to invest and trade stocks and options by replaying real market moments with real tickers, synthetic prices and simulated money, then coaching each trade with AI. It launches free, with no payments, all four levels and a simulated live market, for about **$81 a month** at 1,000 active learners, because it pays for no market data. A paid plan is planned for a later phase.

- **Core loop:** short lesson, then a replay modeled on a real event such as March 2020, compressed into minutes, then an AI debrief.
- **Prices:** real US stocks and ETFs by ticker and name, with every price generated by a market model calibrated to each stock's historical behavior, so there is no data subscription or feed.
- **Options:** earned through four gated levels, from stocks and ETFs up to spreads, priced with a labeled Black-Scholes model.
- **Stack:** one Flutter codebase for iOS, Android and the web, built with Google's official Flutter and Dart agent skills, plus Firebase and Gemini 4 Hydrogen server-side.
- **Safety:** no real money, ads, chat, data selling or real names; teen defaults follow current state laws.
- **Only real cost driver:** AI debriefs, which scale with usage.
- **Recommended name:** Ticker Camp, pending a trademark clearance search.

## Goals and non-goals

The product succeeds when a 13-year-old with no market knowledge can, within a few months, explain and safely use stocks, ETFs and the four common options structures, and has practiced them through modeled versions of real market crashes and rallies.

**Goals**

1. Teach investing and trading by doing. Every concept is practiced in a replay modeled on a real market moment before the learner moves on.
2. Make options understandable. Payoff diagrams, greeks and risk are shown visually, and access is earned level by level.
3. Compress time. A replay turns weeks of real market history into minutes of play, so learners feel volatility, drawdowns and recoveries.
4. Explain every trade. An AI debrief after each replay tells the learner what went well, what went wrong and what to study next.
5. Be safe for teens by default. No real money, no chat, no ads, no data selling and the minimum personal data.
6. Feel world-class. Modern, elegant, fast apps on iPhone, Android and the web, with a design system of their own.
7. Keep recurring costs as close to zero as possible: no market data subscriptions, and AI spend capped.

**Non-goals for version 1**

1. No real-money trading, brokerage links or account aggregation.
2. No personalized investment advice, stock picks or signals.
3. No teacher, classroom or school features.
4. No chat, DMs, comments, public profiles or user-generated content.
5. No crypto, futures, forex or international markets.
6. No licensed market data, real-time or delayed quotes, or real options chains. Tickers and company names are real; every price is synthetic and labeled.
7. No ads and no ad SDKs, ever.

## Level curriculum

Four levels unlock in order, loosely mirroring how US brokers approve options trading in tiers. Brokers number their tiers differently, so the app explains that its levels are a teaching ladder, not any broker's official scheme.

| Level | Unlocks | Core lessons | Unlock test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 Stocks and ETFs | Buy and sell shares and ETFs; market, limit and stop orders | What a share is, how prices move, bid and ask, order types, diversification, index funds, risk and position sizing, drawdowns, long-term compounding | Open to everyone at signup |
| L2 Covered calls and cash-secured puts | Sell calls against 100 shares held; sell puts with cash set aside | What an option is, strike and expiry, premium, assignment, income strategies, giving up upside | L1 quiz at 80% or better, plus a passed L1 replay challenge |
| L3 Long calls and puts | Buy calls and puts | Leverage, time decay, implied volatility, the greeks (delta, gamma, theta, vega), why most short-dated options expire worthless | L2 quiz at 80% or better, plus a passed L2 replay challenge |
| L4 Spreads | Vertical debit and credit spreads; later iron condors and calendars | Defined risk, max loss and max gain, breakevens, choosing strikes, managing and closing early | L3 quiz at 80% or better, plus a passed L3 replay challenge |

**Unlock rules**

1. Each quiz has 10 to 15 questions drawn from a bank, so retakes differ. A failed quiz can be retaken after a short lesson review.
2. A replay challenge has a goal and a risk rule, for example "end the March 2020 replay with less than a 15% drawdown while holding at least three positions." Passing teaches discipline, not just profit.
3. Uncovered (naked) option selling is never offered, because its losses are unlimited and it is not appropriate for beginners.
4. Levels never re-lock, but the debrief can recommend a refresher lesson.

**Lesson format**

Each lesson is 3 to 5 minutes: a short read with an interactive diagram, two or three check questions, and a one-tap "try it" that opens a short replay seeded for that concept. All lesson text is written and reviewed by people; the AI is used only for debriefs.

**Lesson plan**

The curriculum is 33 core lessons of 3 to 5 minutes plus 4 two-minute reality checks, about 141 minutes of reading in all. Each lesson opens with one line from a single running analogy, a farm and its harvest, then switches to real tickers and numbers: one goal, one diagram the learner can drag (price, days left or strike) with real dollar amounts, and one short replay to try it. No other metaphors are used. The glossary at the end gives every term a one-line "think of it like". The full lesson plan is in [Trading Simulator Lesson Plan](lesson-plan.md) (Nov 27, 2026).

## Replay engine

The replay engine plays a curated slice of real market history on the device or in the browser, compressing days into seconds, and the server re-runs the learner's order log to score challenges. It is deterministic: the same scenario and the same orders always give the same result on every platform.

**Scenarios**

A scenario is a hand-built lesson modeled on a real market moment. Launch candidates are the 2008 financial crisis, the March 2020 COVID crash and rebound, the January 2021 meme-stock squeeze, the 2022 rate-hike bear market and the 2000 dot-com bust. Each scenario uses real stocks and ETFs that traded at the time, shown by ticker and name (for example bank stocks in 2008 or airline stocks in 2020), but their prices come from the market model below, not from licensed data.

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| id, title, story | Name and a short narrative shown before play, with no spoilers |
| level | Minimum level needed to play |
| universe | 5 to 30 real stocks and ETFs from that period, always including a broad market ETF, each with its sector and its calibrated sensitivity to the market |
| window | Start and end dates; daily bars for long windows, 1-minute or 5-minute bars for crash days |
| calibration | The event facts the model must reproduce (see Market model) |
| seed | Fixed random seed, so every learner sees the same path |
| startingCash | Usually $10,000 or $100,000 of simulated money |
| objectives | Goal plus risk rule, for example max drawdown or minimum positions |
| newsBeats | Dated, factual headlines written by the team and shown as time passes |
| tier | Free to every learner at launch; a paid library is possible later |

Each scenario ships as a compressed pack of pre-generated bars, dividends, a modeled risk-free rate and the model's volatility series, stored in Cloud Storage. The iOS and Android apps download the pack on first play and cache it; the web app caches it through its service worker, so it plays offline there too.

**Market model**

Every price is synthetic: it is generated by a market model calibrated to how each real stock and the market as a whole have historically behaved, so the app needs no data subscription or feed.

1. **Calibration targets per event:** publicly reported facts such as the market's and each featured stock's peak-to-trough decline, how many days it took, how long the recovery lasted, and how high reported volatility ran. These come from news reports, company filings and reference sources the team cites in the scenario file, not from licensed price data.
2. **Long-run behavior:** average yearly return and volatility, fat-tailed daily moves, volatility that clusters (calm stretches and stormy ones), and stocks moving together more in a crash.
3. **Generator:** a market factor with regime switching (calm, stressed, crash) and occasional jumps, plus each stock's own parameters: its sensitivity to the market (beta), its volatility, its dividend and how it behaved in the event. Parameters are rounded into bands (for example "high-beta tech") rather than copied from any dataset. Each scenario is generated offline with a fixed seed, checked against its calibration targets, reviewed by a person and frozen.
4. **Labels:** every scenario says "Modeled on the March 2020 crash" (or its event), and every price, chart and order ticket says "Simulated price." A one-line note on each ticker screen says that prices are simulated and the company has no connection to the app.
5. **What this gives up:** the paths are faithful in shape and size, not in day-by-day detail, so a learner who checks a real chart will see different numbers. The labels and a short lesson on "why simulated prices differ" make that a teaching point rather than a surprise.
6. **Real names, used carefully:** tickers and company names appear as plain text only, with no logos, brand colors or product images, and nothing that suggests a company sponsors or endorses the app. This follows the nominative-use test from New Kids on the Block v. News America Publishing (9th Cir. 1992): use the name only as needed to identify the company, and imply no endorsement ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominative_use)). Courts have treated tickers as an extension of a company's trademark mainly when a rival used a confusingly similar ticker or brand ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/-/media/files/publications/2017/12/stocktickerstrademarksandthepotentialforconflict.pdf)), which this app does not do. Counsel should confirm before launch.

**Time compression**

1. The learner picks a speed: 1 trading day per 10, 30 or 60 seconds for daily scenarios, or 1 trading minute per second on intraday crash days.
2. The clock pauses automatically on each news beat and whenever an order fills or a stop triggers.
3. The thumb-scrubbable timeline lets the learner rewind to review, but orders can only be placed at the live head, so there is no trading on hindsight.
4. A free "study mode" after the replay ends lets the learner scrub the whole window and see what happened next.

**Accounts and orders**

The account is a cash account: no margin and no short selling of stock in version 1. Order types are market, limit, stop and stop-limit, each day-only or good-till-cancelled. Option orders are market or limit. Collateral is enforced: a cash-secured put reserves strike × 100 in cash, a covered call needs 100 shares, and a spread reserves its maximum loss.

**Fills and costs**

1. Market orders fill at the next bar's open plus slippage. Slippage is half a modeled spread, set by a liquidity tier (for example 1 basis point for the broad market fund, 5 to 20 basis points for small companies) and widened when the bar is volatile.
2. Limit orders fill when the bar's range crosses the limit, at the limit price. Gaps fill at the open, which teaches gap risk.
3. Stops trigger when the bar crosses the stop and then fill like a market order, so a crash day shows real stop slippage.
4. There are no partial fills in version 1.
5. Stock commissions are $0. Options carry a configurable $0.65 per contract, so learners see that costs add up. This is a teaching default, not any broker's fee.
6. Dividends are credited on ex-dates, and splits are already adjusted in the bars.

**Modeled options**

Replays have no real historical options chains, so prices are modeled with Black-Scholes and every option price carries a "Modeled" badge. Expirations follow the real calendar (monthly third-Friday expiries and weeklies where they existed), and strikes use standard increments around the spot price.

```latex
C = S e^{-qT} N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2), \quad P = K e^{-rT} N(-d_2) - S e^{-qT} N(-d_1)
\\
d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + (r - q + \sigma^2/2)T}{\sigma\sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}
```

- S is the modeled underlying price at the current bar, K the strike, T the time to expiry in years, r the modeled risk-free rate, and q the dividend yield.
- σ (volatility) is the market model's own volatility for that instrument on that date, so option prices rise when the modeled market gets stormy. A simple skew adds a few volatility points to out-of-the-money puts, so crash protection costs more, as it does in real markets.
- The modeled bid and ask sit around the model price with a spread of the larger of $0.05 or a percentage of the price, wider for far-out strikes.
- Greeks (delta, gamma, theta, vega) come from the same formula and are shown on every position, with plain-language tooltips.
- Exercise and assignment happen only at expiry in version 1: in-the-money options settle automatically. A lesson explains that real American-style options can be assigned early.

**Scoring and anti-cheat**

The device sends the order log, not the result. A Cloud Function replays the same bar pack with the same rules and computes return, max drawdown and objective pass or fail. Only server-computed results count for unlocks and leaderboards.

## Live paper trading

Live mode is a simulated market of real US stocks and ETFs that runs in real time during US market hours, with synthetic prices generated by the same market model as replays instead of a data feed. Everyone trades the same live path, and every price screen says "Simulated price," so nobody mistakes it for today's real quote.

1. **Universe:** about 100 well-known US stocks and ETFs across sectors and sizes, including a broad market ETF, each with its own calibrated beta, volatility and dividend. Tickers and names come from the SEC's free [company\_tickers.json](https://www.sec.gov/os/accessing-edgar-data) file, refreshed by a build script within the SEC's fair-access rules (a declared user agent, at most 10 requests a second). Each stock starts a season at a round reference price and then moves by the model, so it will drift away from its real price, and the app says so.
2. **Prices:** a scheduled Cloud Function advances the market model by one minute during market hours, using long-run historical behavior (calm days, occasional storms, rare crashes), and writes compact price documents to Firestore. Apps listen to those documents, so cost does not grow with every user's screen.
3. **Fills:** orders are queued in Firestore and filled by a Cloud Function against the next tick, with the replay engine's slippage and limit rules. Orders placed while the market is closed fill at the next session's first tick.
4. **Options:** modeled with the replay engine's Black-Scholes model from the simulated price and the model's volatility, labeled "Modeled."
5. **Accounts:** one live account per learner, starting at $100,000 simulated, with a reset allowed once a week. Gated levels apply exactly as in replays.
6. **Events:** modeled dividends, earnings-day jumps and the occasional market shock keep the live market educational. The model never copies today's real headlines or real earnings, so a simulated move never claims to be what a real company did.
7. **Guardrails:** live mode shows no buy or sell suggestions, no "trending" lists and no price alerts that read like trade signals.

## AI debrief

After each finished replay, and on demand for a closed live trade, Gemini 4 Hydrogen writes a short coaching debrief from a structured summary of what the learner did. The model never sees personal data and never recommends real securities to buy or sell.

**Flow**

1. The app finishes a replay and the server scores it (see Replay engine).
2. A Cloud Function builds a compact JSON summary: scenario, level, orders with timestamps, prices, position sizes, drawdown, outcome, the objective's pass or fail, and the concepts the scenario teaches. No name, email or birthdate is included; age is passed only as "teen" or "adult" to set the tone.
3. The function calls Gemini 4 Hydrogen (gemini-4-hydrogen) server-side with a fixed system prompt and asks for structured output: three things that went well, up to three mistakes tied to specific moments, one concept to study next (linked to a lesson), and a one-line takeaway.
4. The function validates the output against a schema and a safety filter that blocks stock picks, "you should buy" language and anything that reads as advice. On failure it retries once, then shows a template debrief.
5. The debrief is stored with the replay result so re-opening it costs nothing.

**Daily caps**

| Plan | Debriefs per day | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free (everyone at launch) | 1 | Additional finished replays get the template debrief. |
| Paid (later phase) | 10 | Enough for heavy daily use; protects against runaway cost. |

Caps are enforced in the Cloud Function with a per-user daily counter in Firestore, not in the app. A global monthly budget alert in Google Cloud and a kill switch in Remote Config fall back to template debriefs if spend spikes. Prompt inputs are small structured JSON, so each debrief stays a few thousand tokens; the per-debrief and monthly prices are in the Costs section.

## Challenges and leaderboards

Every week, all learners can play the same replay challenge, and server-scored results rank them on leaderboards under generated nicknames. There is no chat, no DMs, no comments and no free-text anywhere another user can see.

1. **Weekly challenge:** a new scenario each Monday at 9 AM Eastern, open for 7 days, one scored attempt per learner (practice runs are unlimited but unscored). Every learner plays the same challenge, now and after a paid plan arrives.
2. **Scoring:** a risk-adjusted score, not raw return, so reckless all-in bets do not win. For example, return divided by max drawdown, with the objective required to qualify.
3. **Nicknames:** generated from word lists (for example "Calm Otter 482"), rerollable but never typed by the user, so no real names or offensive handles appear.
4. **Leaderboards:** global, by level, and friends. Each shows rank, nickname, score and level badge only.
5. **Friends:** each learner has an invite code. Entering a friend's code adds both to each other's friends board. Either can remove the other. No contact-list upload, no friend suggestions, no search by name.
6. **Teen defaults:** learners under 18 appear on the global board only under their nickname, never with an avatar photo or location, and can hide themselves from the global board entirely.
7. **Rewards:** badges and streaks only. There are no cash prizes, gift cards or sweepstakes, which avoids contest and gambling rules.

## Privacy, teen safety and disclaimers

The app collects almost nothing: a sign-in identifier, a birthdate used only to decide the age bucket, a generated nickname and learning activity. With no ads, no data sale, no chat and no precise location, most teen privacy rules are met by design; the remaining duties are listed here. State-law status below comes partly from law-firm summaries, so counsel should confirm it before launch.

**Data**

1. Signup asks for the birthdate in a neutral way (no hint of the "right" answer). Under-13s are turned away and nothing is kept. The amended COPPA rule's compliance date was April 22, 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)).
2. After signup, the app stores only the birth year and an age bucket (13 to 15, 16 to 17, 18+), not the full birthdate. No real name, phone, contacts, photos or location are collected.
3. Data is used only to run the service, keep it secure and meet legal duties, which matches New York's "strictly necessary" standard for known minors ([NY AG guidance](https://ag.ny.gov/child-data-protection-act-guidance)).
4. Nothing is sold or shared for advertising, so California's opt-in rule for under-16s is never triggered ([Cal. Civ. Code 1798.120](https://law.justia.com/codes/california/2022/code-civ/division-3/part-4/title-1-81-5/section-1798-120/)).
5. Learners can export and delete their data in the app. Deleted accounts are erased within 30 days.
6. A short, teen-readable privacy notice sits beside the full policy.

**State app-store age laws**

According to a [Squire Patton Boggs summary from Nov 19, 2026](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2026/11/app-store-age-verification-laws-your-questions-answered-updated/), Texas SB 2420 has been enforceable since June 4, 2026; Utah's duties start May 6, 2027; California AB 1043 starts January 1, 2027; and Louisiana's starts July 1, 2027. The app therefore:

1. Reads the store's age signal at launch: Apple's [Declared Age Range API](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=f5zj08ey) and Google's [Play Age Signals API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691), and treats it as the main indicator of age.
2. Rates the app honestly, and notifies the stores before any "significant change," which in Utah includes adding purchases, so adding the paid plan later needs that notice.
3. Keeps age data to the minimum and deletes it when it is no longer needed.
4. On the web, where no store age signal exists, relies on the neutral birthdate gate at signup and applies the same teen defaults by age bucket.

**Design duties for teens**

Colorado requires consent before features that significantly increase use, such as streaks, for known minors, and Vermont's design code (January 1, 2027) adds a duty of care against compulsive use ([Troutman summary](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2025/09/analyzing-the-2025-childrens-privacy-laws-and-regulations/), [FPF on Vermont](https://fpf.org/blog/vermont-and-nebraska-diverging-experiments-in-state-age-appropriate-design-codes/)). For learners under 18 the app therefore:

1. Turns streaks and streak reminders off by default; teens can turn them on.
2. Sends no push notifications, on mobile or the web, between 9 PM and 8 AM local time, and no more than one a day.
3. Shows a gentle break prompt after 45 minutes of continuous play.
4. Keeps the profile private: nickname only, no avatar photo, and an option to hide from the global leaderboard.
5. Never shows real-money trading offers, broker links or affiliate referrals.

**Store age rating and financial-app rules**

Apple's tiers are 4+, 9+, 13+, 16+ and 18+; leaderboards count as "contests," and simulated gambling means betting without real money ([Apple age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/age-ratings)). The target rating is 13+. Simulated stock and options trading is not betting, but no official guidance says so, so the questionnaire answers should be checked with counsel. Apple requires real trading apps to come from licensed institutions (3.2.1(viii)) and bans binary options apps (3.2.2(viii)); the listing and first screen must make "simulated money, no real trading" unmistakable. On Google Play, the Financial features declaration is completed as an app with no financial features ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13849271?hl=en)), to be confirmed at submission.

The web app has no store rating or store review, so its landing page shows the same 13+ age gate and the "simulated money, no real trading" message before signup, and its web manifest and page metadata describe it as an education app.

**Not investment advice**

The app gives impersonal, general education and never personalized recommendations, which keeps it away from investment-adviser status; publisher-style content has been treated as outside the Advisers Act ([Greenberg Traurig on Seeking Alpha](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/8/no-need-for-seeking-alpha-to-seek-registration)). Counsel should confirm this, especially for AI debriefs. This disclaimer appears at signup, in settings, on every debrief and in the store listing:

> For educational purposes only. All trading in this app uses simulated money; no real securities are bought or sold. Nothing here is investment, tax or legal advice. All prices are modeled from historical market behavior and are not real market quotes. Past or simulated results do not guarantee future results.

In the web app, the same disclaimer also appears on the landing page and in the footer of every screen.

## Architecture

One Flutter codebase ships native iOS and Android apps and an installable web app (PWA), and Firebase runs everything server-side: auth, data, scoring, the simulated live market and AI calls, plus payments once the paid phase arrives. No secret or vendor key ever lives in any app.

**App (Flutter and Dart)**

1. Flutter stable with Dart 3, Material 3 and a custom theme layer (see Design). State with Riverpod or Bloc, routing with go\_router, both chosen to match the official Flutter agent skills' architecture guidance.
2. Charts, payoff diagrams and the replay timeline are custom-painted widgets (CustomPainter), so they look like nothing else and stay at 60 to 120 frames per second on phones and at 60 in the web app, which renders them with Flutter's CanvasKit or Skwasm renderer.
3. The replay engine is a pure Dart package shared by the iOS, Android and web apps and the scoring server, so every client and the server compute identical results.
4. Offline: downloaded scenario packs play without a connection in the iOS and Android apps and in the installed web app; results sync when back online.
5. Purchases (later phase, not in version 1): native StoreKit and Google Play Billing through a Flutter in-app purchase plugin on mobile, and Stripe Checkout with Stripe Billing on the web. A Cloud Function will verify store receipts and Stripe webhooks and write one entitlement per learner to Firestore. The launch build has no purchase code; each learner record carries a plan field set to free, which the debrief caps read.
6. Web app (PWA), set up the way the guest experience app is: a Flutter web build served from Firebase Hosting with a single-page rewrite, no-cache headers on the entry files, a web manifest (standalone display, maskable icons) and Flutter's service worker, so the app installs from Chrome and from Safari's Add to Home Screen.
7. The web build uses `flutter build web --wasm` (Flutter 3.24 or later); browsers that lack the WebAssembly features it needs get the JavaScript build from the same command automatically ([Flutter docs](https://docs.flutter.dev/platform-integration/web/wasm)).
8. Web push uses Firebase Cloud Messaging with a messaging service worker. On iPhone and iPad, web push reaches only a web app added to the Home Screen, from iOS 16.4 ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados)), so the app offers an "Install the app" sheet there and asks for notification permission only from a tap.
9. The replay engine runs in the browser too, so scenario packs cached by the service worker play offline in the installed web app.

Auth is free to 50,000 MAU, then $0.0055 per MAU ([Identity Platform pricing](https://cloud.google.com/identity-platform/pricing)). Functions bill as Cloud Run with a monthly free tier ([Cloud Run pricing](https://cloud.google.com/run/pricing)). Cloud Scheduler gives 3 free jobs, then $0.10 per job a month ([Scheduler pricing](https://cloud.google.com/scheduler/pricing)). Analytics and Crashlytics are free ([Firebase pricing](https://firebase.google.com/pricing)).

**Store and payment fees (later phase)**

None of these apply at launch beyond the developer accounts, because nothing is sold. When the paid plan arrives: Apple keeps 15% of subscriptions for members of the Small Business Program (under $1M a year), and Google, in the US since June 30, 2026, takes about 15% on subscriptions (a 10% service fee plus a 5% billing fee) (Play service fees). On the web, Stripe charges 2.9% plus 30 cents per card payment, plus 0.7% for Stripe Billing, about $0.59 on a $7.99 month, with no monthly fee ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing)).

**Funding and break-even**

Version 1 earns no revenue, so the publisher pays its \~$81 a month. When a paid plan arrives at, say, $7.99 a month, about $6.79 reaches us after the 15% store commission and about $7.40 after Stripe fees on the web, so about 12 paying learners would cover launch-scale costs and about 113 would cover \~$762 at 10k MAU. The pricing itself is an open question.

**What I could and could not verify**

1. Domains were checked against live registry availability on Nov 27, 2026. "Free" means unregistered at that moment, not reserved; premium pricing was not checked.
2. App store checks were web searches of apps.apple.com and play.google.com, not in-store searches, so a new or unindexed app could be missed.
3. I could not reach the USPTO search system from here. The trademark column comes from web searches of trademark indexes such as Justia, which can lag the USPTO. ## Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Modeled prices feel fake or teach the wrong intuitions about real markets | Learners don't trust it | Calibrate each replay to cited public facts (drawdown, duration, recovery, volatility), test against long-run statistics, human review of every scenario, and a lesson on how real markets differ |
| Learners mistake a synthetic price for a real quote | Confusion, or a real-money decision made on a fake price | "Simulated price" on every price, chart and order ticket, stock prices that start each season at round reference values, and a lesson on why they differ |
| A company objects to its name or ticker beside synthetic prices, or claims implied endorsement | Takedown request or legal cost | Plain-text names only, no logos or brand colors, a no-affiliation note on ticker screens, and counsel review before launch |
| A historical ticker now belongs to a different company (for example after a delisting) | Wrong company named in a replay | Every replay shows the company's name as of the event's date, from the scenario file, not today's ticker list |
| AI cost runs above plan (thinking tokens, heavy users) | Monthly cost climbs fast | Server-side caps, thinking budget, monthly budget alert, Remote Config kill switch, Flash-Lite fallback |
| AI says something that reads as investment advice or is wrong | Trust and regulatory risk | Structured output, advice filter, template fallback, human review of a sample each week |
| Learners treat it as gambling or chase risky trades | Harm to teens | Risk-adjusted scoring, no cash prizes, lessons on loss, no naked options, no leverage |
| Modeled option prices mislead | Wrong intuitions | "Modeled" badge everywhere and a lesson on how real chains differ |
| Store review questions the age rating | Launch delay | Complete age-rating questionnaires carefully; with no purchases at launch, the store purchase rules don't apply yet |
| iPhone web app limits (push only after Add to Home Screen, storage that Safari can clear) | Fewer reminders, re-downloaded packs | "Install the app" sheet, progress kept in Firestore not the browser, and the iOS app recommended to iPhone users |
| State teen privacy or app-store age laws change | Rework | Minimal data by design; use the platforms' age-signal APIs and the signup birthdate gate on the web; review laws before launch |
| No revenue at launch | The publisher carries \~$81 a month, rising with usage | Keep the AI cap and the Flash-Lite fallback ready, and add the paid plan once usage justifies it |
| Name conflicts with an existing trademark | Rebrand cost | Attorney clearance search before buying domains or launching |

## Open questions

- When should the paid plan arrive, and at what price? This spec uses $7.99 for break-even math only.
- Which name? Ticker Camp is the recommendation, pending a trademark clearance search.
- Does counsel agree that showing real tickers and company names beside synthetic, clearly labeled prices is nominative use that needs no license from the companies, and that a model calibrated to public facts needs no market data license? If counsel prefers more distance, the fallback is the earlier plan of fictional tickers modeled on real companies.
- Which public sources set each stock's calibration bands and each replay's event facts, and who signs off that each modeled replay matches its event closely enough? If public facts prove too thin, a one-time purchase of a historical dataset whose license allows derived models is the fallback; it would be a one-time cost, not a feed.
- When payments arrive, should the web app offer a parent-pays checkout for teens, and how would a parent's approval be verified there?
- Who writes and reviews the lesson content and the news beats for each replay?
- Should teens be able to hide from the global leaderboard by default, rather than opt out?
- Which entity publishes the apps (an existing company or a new one), since the developer accounts, and later the Stripe account, need a legal owner?
